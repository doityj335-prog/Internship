90-second version (add after the above)

What I bring is the ability to read a technology the way an engineer does and then ask the questions an investor needs answered: where the yield is lost, how long customer qualification takes, and who controls the capacity. At Focus, lifting reliability-test yield from about 98% to 99% sounds small, but it halved the failure rate on our highest-volume product line. That taught me to look at the process step where losses happen, not just the headline number. Before my PhD I also worked at Alpha Cen, a startup, where I evaluated three foundries on cost, capability and lead time to get an AR optics prototype made. 4. Industry report in 90 seconds

My core view is that the bottleneck in AI computing is shifting from compute to data movement. Once each electrical lane reaches 200G, passive copper only works reliably over about one meter, so optics has to move inside the rack. I look at it through three questions.
Why now? Scale-up interconnect between GPUs is moving from copper to optics, and hyperscaler capex keeps rising.
Where is the value? Gross margins are highest upstream: leading optical-chip makers are around 80%, versus mid-to-high 40s for module makers. China leads the world in modules, but about 95% of high-speed optical chips above 25G per lane still come from overseas, and DSPs are mostly imported too.
Where should capital go? My first pick is the process bottlenecks of co-packaged optics: fiber coupling, external laser sources and packaging and test. Those steps decide whether CPO can scale, and they don't depend on a single technology route winning. Micro-LED links are worth tracking in stages, but they're not ready for a big bet yet. The main risk is that private-market valuations are running ahead of real customer adoption.

5. Mapping report in 90 seconds

Instead of listing companies, I started from industry constraints. There are five: indium phosphide laser supply, modulation bandwidth, fiber coupling, reliability and power. For each one I mapped two to four technology routes and the teams behind them, then ranked who to follow up with. Two observations stood out. First, incumbents are buying capabilities: Marvell acquired Celestial AI for about 3.25 billion dollars, and Molex bought Teramount, a fiber-coupling startup, for about 430 million. Second, in Asia, university spin-out platforms in Hong Kong and Singapore are very active, which is a way to find teams before they become companies.

If they say "many of these are too early for us"

I agree. I originally built it from an early-stage VC perspective. If I re-screen it for Tai's model, commercialized companies with a cross-border angle, I'd split it into three tiers. Look now: LESSENGERS in Korea, which already has products and is co-developing 1.6T modules with POET; PhotonicX AI, headquartered in Singapore with a Suzhou entity opened in 2025; and HyperLight, which is at Series C with a volume foundry partnership with UMC. Good fit for a China-scaling play: LightSpeed Photonics, a fabless company that needs packaging partners, and China has the densest module and packaging capacity in the world. Track only: research projects like the Solgaard group at Stanford or barium titanate materials.

6. Likely questions: short English answers

QuestionAnswer
Why is EML hard to make?"An EML integrates a DFB laser and an electro-absorption modulator on one indium phosphide chip. That needs high-quality quantum-well epitaxy and regrowth, so yields are low and supply is concentrated in a few Japanese and US vendors."
Why separate the laser and the modulator?"Four reasons: you can choose a higher-bandwidth modulator material; one CW laser can feed four channels, which saves indium phosphide; the laser is the most heat-sensitive part, so an external laser source improves reliability and can be swapped; and the modulator can use CMOS capacity. EML won't disappear, though. 200G EML is still key for 1.6T pluggables."
What makes fiber coupling hard?"A size mismatch: single-mode fiber has a core of about 9 microns, and a silicon waveguide is under one micron. You need mode conversion and sub-micron alignment, usually active alignment, which is slow. Adhesive shrinkage and thermal cycling hurt long-term reliability, and with 16 or more fibers in an array, one bad channel ruins the whole assembly."
Chip vs. engine vs. module?"The chip is the bare die. The optical engine adds drivers, TIAs and attached fibers and does the electrical-to-optical conversion. The module wraps the engine with a DSP, a PCB and a housing in a pluggable form factor. CPO essentially removes the module and puts the engine next to the switch chip, so value shifts toward engines, packaging and chips."
Micro-LED interconnect?"Single devices and small links have been demonstrated, but full links and volume manufacturing haven't been proven. The biggest uncertainty is whether it gets designed into mainstream GPU and switch architectures. Display makers moving in may gain a second growth curve from their GaN epitaxy capability."
Why do standards matter in diligence?"CPO interfaces aren't standardized yet. Being active in standards bodies like OIF decides whether you can become a second source, can tilt the spec toward your design, and is indirect evidence of real customer engagement."
How do you think about yield and cost?"Total yield is the product of every step: five steps at 90% each give you about 59%. In CPO, 16 channels at 99% each give an engine yield of only about 85%, and a bad engine can scrap an expensive switch package. Gross margin depends on whether the cost curve falls faster than the price curve."
Biggest barrier for overseas companies entering China?"Export controls and cross-border investment reviews such as US outbound investment rules and CFIUS. I'm still learning the details, but I'd treat that as the first screening filter. European, Korean, Japanese or Singaporean targets may be structurally easier."
Conflict of interest with Porotech?"I disclosed it in the report: my PhD supervisor is a co-founder. It's an access advantage, but if we pursued it I'd fully disclose or step back."
Did you use AI?"Yes, for search and first drafts, which made me faster. The framework, judgments and source checks are mine, and I can trace every number back to its source."

7. Methodology and transferable skills

My method is bottleneck → technology routes → where value sits → judgment, diligence questions and risks. For data, I cross-check filings, company announcements, broker research and trade media; I flag different definitions, and I drop numbers I can't trust. Four things transfer directly to due diligence. One: I translate technical metrics into business variables: yield into cost and margin, reliability into qualification time. Two: I distinguish hero samples from production data. Three: I can assess the supply chain and capacity, because I've evaluated foundries and transferred a process into mass production. Four: the framework is reusable. I applied it to 800-volt DC power and solid-state transformers for AI data centers, and my conclusion was that the direction is certain, the route isn't, and the hype is ahead of deployment.

9. Finance basics in English

  • Three statements: net income flows into retained earnings; depreciation is added back in operating cash flow; capex sits in investing cash flow and builds PP&E.
  • Free cash flow = operating cash flow − capex. Tencent's FCF turned negative in Q2 because of compute spending.
  • Valuation: P/E for profitable companies; EV/Revenue for high-growth, pre-profit ones; EV/EBITDA for capital-intensive ones; DCF is very sensitive to growth and WACC. Early-stage VC relies more on comparable rounds and exit-value back-solving.
  • Post-money = pre-money + new investment. Ownership = investment ÷ post-money (9 pre + 1 in = 10 post → 10%). Also: dilution, lead investor, liquidation preference, anti-dilution, redemption rights, MOIC, IRR.
  • Eoptolink model exercise: "Revenue is volume times ASP, split by 400G, 800G and 1.6T. ASPs fall every year, so gross margin is driven by product mix. The most sensitive assumptions are the 1.6T ramp and the annual price decline. This was my own practice model."
